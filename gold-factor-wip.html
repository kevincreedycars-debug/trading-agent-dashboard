1. Where the questions stand

50 of 50 answers in — the question round closed on 30 September 2026, questions 1–50 with no gap and nothing left on a default.

The round closed on 30 September 2026: all fifty answers are in, each one the user's own reply, and the last ten — the two remaining measurement rules and the shape of the finished page — came back in one message. What each of them settles:

  • 41 — yes. The outcome is counted from the next session's open after the state is seen, so a Friday state starts from Monday's open and no row counts the session the state was seen in.
  • 42 — yes. The state must be knowable before the session it is scored against, so nothing in a count may use that day's own prices. The same reply asks whether the work can measure both before the session and during it; the during-session stream is accepted in principle and its shape is the one point still open, set out below.
  • 43 — yes. One page listing every factor with its raw movement numbers and no direction claim. The same reply says the calls come in the next step, which is answer 39's later stage and not this page.
  • 44 — yes. Every published number carries its own day count and the floor that produced it.
  • 45 — yes. A ranked list stays a list: never the winner, never the pick.
  • 46 — yes. The page states how many factors and how many cuts were examined in total, so the best cell is never read as the only one.
  • 47 — yes. The direction layer is rebuilt only after the movement layer is agreed.
  • 48 — yes. The answers are written down as decisions before any lane starts.
  • 49 — yes: the full page beside the accuracy panel, not the minimum version appended to the scorecard page. This draft is that page, published early on the user's own instruction.
  • 50 — yes. The movement screen comes before anything else is built.

The one point still open, from answer 42. Measuring during the session as well as before it is agreed in principle, and it is kept as a second, separately labelled stream, never a single mixed percentage: the before-session stream keeps answer 41's next-open rule, while a during-session state is timed from the bar that first shows it and measured to that session's close, with the same window extended to the next session's close printed beside it. The two are never pooled, because their windows are different lengths and they overlap, and the looks count covers both. Two details of that stream are with the user, with the defaults that stand if no answer comes: the trigger uses the hourly bars already accepted in this work, and the headline window ends at that session's close. Nothing on this page uses it yet — no during-session number has been measured here.

Two readings, and they pull opposite ways. The movement stage is almost always “yes” at 0.50 L2L — 97.89% of days — so at that size the range cannot sort the days. The direction stage is the weak part: on the days that moved one way only, the call named that side 45.63% of the time while simply saying “up” every day would have been right 61.13% of the time. It is the same finding the accepted verdict already carries (call right at the close 46.32% against 56.84% up-closes), read here through the user's own criterion. Days where both sides reached the range are left out of the matched count, because a call is right either way once both sides are touched.

Read plainly: the move is the same-day part and it stays true — the range is reached on 97.89% of days and on 99.82% of five-day windows, so “will it move” is certain and useless for sorting days. Direction never arrives with time: the call names the side 45.63% at one session and 44.32% at five, while always saying up improves from 61.13% to 67.05%. And the move is handed back rather than carried: of the calls that reached the range, 68.49% were still on the right side at the one-session close but only 55.20% at the five-session close.

Gold's median day range ran from 1.32% to 3.12% across the period, so a fixed percentage of price is a different bet each year, while the L2L form is the same bet. That is why the user's ranges are the ones the tables use, and why the fixed rows stay visible only as labelled sensitivities.

3.5 Nothing clears the bar yet

The accepted bar is a 60% hit rate and at least 100 days, with the sign holding in every year. On the session — the horizon this project treats as primary — no VIX state clears 60%; the closest is 59.21% and the drift alone is 55.81%. One row touches the bar at the week horizon at 60.51%, and it must not be presented as a result: it is one of twelve looks on data already spent, and the same state's session number is 52.86%, below gold's own drift. The 60% figure is also not a 60% expectation, because the yearly drift moves a lot: 51.54% (2023), 59.16% (2024), 60.15% (2025), 50.83% (2026).

4. What comes next

  1. The measurement run. The band rules are written down first — VIX level bands, both VIX streams, the direction document's own thresholds — then the run measures every state against the movement ranges, prints the 1/2/3/5 ladder, and produces one page of raw splits with no direction claim in it. Each state is measured twice: once with everything knowable before the session (the headline count, which starts at the next open) and once for states that only appear during the session, timed from the bar that first shows them and kept in its own labelled block. The two are never mixed into one percentage.
  2. The factor table, then the page. Every factor gets one light (“worth watching today” or “nothing here today”) with one short plain reason line underneath carrying the size of the move and the day count — no score out of 100, no ranking, no winner. The page sits beside the accuracy panel and every number carries its day count and the range that produced it.
  3. Later, and separately agreed: the daily-call logic. The user's stated purpose for this work is to reassemble the algorithm behind the daily calls from what the factors are shown to have done. That is a later step and it is not part of the current work.

This page is the draft of the eventual factor page: it is published now, marked as a draft, and rewritten as the tables are built and each measurement lane is accepted.

5. What this page is not

  • Not a forecast. Nothing here looks forward, and no window is held back for a future test; a date range on this page says which record was read and nothing more.
  • Not a trading result. No spread or slippage is subtracted, and there is no cost-adjusted figure, no profit and loss and no entry, stop or target anywhere in this work.
  • Not a signal. The flags are not traded. Every row is a reading of what the factors did historically — the user's own example: a factor that implies up, on days gold then rose 70% of the time, is a 70% reliable factor, and that is an account of the past rather than a recommendation.
  • Not a verdict on any factor. Nothing clears the interest bar today, and the tables will say so on the page rather than in a footnote.
  • Correlation, not cause. Each state is compared with gold's own behaviour on the same days, never with a coin flip; the factors overlap by construction, one moment can feed several of them, and the counts are not independent trials.
  • Bounded by the data. About 470 days can show a 5-point gap but not confirm it (about 774 are needed), and at these group sizes a real 3-point effect is invisible, so “nothing found” means “nothing large” and not “nothing there”. The record has no flat bucket either — no session closed exactly unchanged — so “a move happened” is a line drawn here, and it always travels with the range that drew it.
  • Not finished. The question round is closed at 50 of 50, no factor table exists yet, and this page is a draft by its own description. One point is still open with the user: how the during-session stream is timed.
