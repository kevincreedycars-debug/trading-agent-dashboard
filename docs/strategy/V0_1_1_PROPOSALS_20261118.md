## 0. Why this is a separate set

1. **v0.1.0 is frozen and stays frozen.** The package declares `USD-L1-25F-H1-v0.1.0`; the screen, the ledger and the
   saved morning records all carry that stamp. `inputs.json` hashes to `144ae500…`, `rows.json` to `47aa93fc…` and the
   received archive to `1c82e904…`; the screen reproduces from the committed artefacts. Editing the frozen rows would
   invalidate every saved decision and every fixture at once, and would make the 2026-11-17 scoring contract describe a
   model that no longer exists.
2. **v0.1.1 would be a new package.** `backtester/packages/USD-L1-25F-H1-v0.1.1/`, its own `inputs.json`, `models.js`,
   fixtures, `expected.json` and digest, with its own version string. v0.1.0 records keep their stamp and remain
   readable; the two are never mixed under one selector.
3. **The scoring contract is not edited.** It was frozen on 2026-11-17 before any holdout scoring
   (`FROZEN_BEFORE_SCORING`). A v0.1.1 candidate is a new candidate and needs its own registered sample, holdout,
   multiplicity and pass/fail plan before any number is scored.
4. **No proposal changes today's counts.** H1 readiness stays 0 of 725 whether these entries are accepted or not,
   because the archive still holds no licensed ICE fixing, no LBMA PM auction and no complete release calendar.


## 1. P-01 · ICE 05:00 timing (review finding H1R-03, severity *defect*)

**This is first because it is the one finding that makes a sealed live result impossible, not merely unproven.**

### What v0.1.0 says

| Where | Text |
| --- | --- |
| `inputs.json` → `ice` | `["ICE", "US Dollar Index spot", "previous eligible London 05:00 fixing to current 05:00, simple return in percent", 0]` — the fourth element is `max_age_hours`, and it is **0** |
| `lib/layer1_backtesting/h1_candidate.js:59-63` | clocks must all parse, `observed <= published <= received`, `received <= cutoff`; otherwise `"<input>: invalid or late clock"`; then `cutoff - observed > max_age_hours` gives `"<input>: stale"` |
| `backtester/packages/USD-L1-25F-H1-v0.1.0/engine.js:15-18` | the same two tests against the same `inputs.json` entry |
| `backtester/packages/USD-L1-25F-H1-v0.1.0/verify.js:10` | the accepted fixture stamps ICE `observed = published = received = 2026-11-16T05:00:00Z`, exactly the cutoff |

With `max_age_hours = 0`, `cutoff - observed <= 0` forces `observed >= cutoff`. The ordering test forces
`received >= observed`, and the late test forces `received <= cutoff`. The only solution is
`observed = published = received = cutoff`, to the millisecond. The fixture passes because it is written to that
identity; a real collector cannot deliver a zero-latency receipt stamped at 05:00:00.000, and a historical archive
cannot either.

The screen records the consequence already: *"Sealed live proof for the ICE vote is impossible under v0.1.0 as
written, and every historical morning stays blocked even if licensed fixings are acquired."*

### What v0.1.1 proposes

**The clock rule.** An eligible ICE row carries three recorded clocks:

| Clock | Rule in v0.1.1 |
| --- | --- |
| `observed_at_utc` | the **05:00 Europe/London fixing instant** of the fixing used. It must be a real 05:00 London stamp (winter and summer offsets both accepted, computed from the tz database, not assumed) |
| `published_at_utc` | the publisher's recorded release stamp for that fixing. ICE releases the 05:00 fixing at 05:00 London, so this normally equals `observed_at_utc`; if the licensed record carries a later official release time, the true stamp is stored |
| `received_at_utc` | the collector's receipt. **Required**, with `published <= received <= cutoff` |

Ordering, finiteness and `received <= cutoff` are unchanged from v0.1.0. What changes is the instant the receipt must

**The maximum age.** The recorded H1R-03 wording proposes 24 hours. A review of the session schedule shows that 24
hours reintroduces a structural block:

- the archive schedules **725 sessions**, 145 complete Monday-to-Friday weeks, first 2024-01-02, last 2026-10-12
  (`run-v1.json`; session gaps are 579 of one day and 145 of three days, counted in `tmp/gaps.out.txt` and recomputed by
  `tmp/monday-gaps-20261118.js`);
- **145 of 725 mornings (20.0%) are Mondays**, whose most recent eligible 05:00 fixing is 72 hours old;
- the archive contains **no holiday gaps at all**, which is itself a defect in the schedule; a real calendar adds
  roughly ten post-holiday mornings a year, and each would also fail a 24-hour cap.

Two options are therefore put to the owner:

| Option | Maximum age | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A, as recorded in H1R-03 | 24 h | up to 20% of mornings can never be eligible, plus every post-holiday morning |
| **B, default** | **96 h** | the same cap the other daily inputs already use (VIX, DGS2, DFII10 and the spread all carry 96 h); Mondays and three-day holiday gaps are reachable, and the "latest eligible fixing" requirement stops the series from silently ageing |

Default if there is no reply: **Option B (96 hours)**, because a rule that structurally excludes a fifth of the sample
cannot be presented as an evidence rule rather than a calendar artefact.

**The value rule.** The return is computed from the latest eligible fixing received at or before the cutoff and the
fixing immediately before it, in percent: `(latest / previous - 1) * 100`. The two fixings must be **distinct dates**;
a repeated fixing would produce a silent 0.00% and a neutral vote bought with a data defect, so it blocks instead.

### What this does not weaken

This is the part that matters, because the ICE change touches the "historical inputs available by decision cutoff"
principle:

1. **Nothing may be received after the cutoff.** `received <= cutoff` is unchanged. A fixing published at 05:00 and
   collected at 05:00:04 is admissible for the *next* morning, never retroactively for the one whose cutoff it missed.
2. **Nothing may be published after the cutoff.** `published <= cutoff`; the true release stamp is stored, and a
   record published later than the cutoff blocks the morning.
3. **Observation may not be later than publication, and the fixing must be a 05:00 London fixing.** An intraday ICE
   print is not the declared instrument and is refused.
4. **No fill, carry-forward, interpolation, proxy or neighbour.** A missing or stale input still blocks the whole
   morning; the integrity gate keeps no fallback (H1R-05, accepted).
5. **The broker DXY proxy stays refused.** `DXY.cash` is not ICE US Dollar Index spot, and the identity test is
   unchanged (H1R-07 and the `dxy_direction_broker_cfd` proxy label).
6. **The other eight inputs keep their own caps** (96 h, 96 h, 96 h, 96 h, 192 h, 96 h, 96 h, 24 h release calendar).
   Only the ICE row changes.
7. **Readiness is not diluted.** Strict readiness stays "every required input present, fresh, clocked, receipted and
   identified". No morning becomes eligible until a licensed fixing record exists that satisfies all of the above.

## 2. P-02 · The front-end as a spread operand (H1R-01, severity *assumption*)

**v0.1.0 records:** *"The DGS2 five-interval change is required by the evidence gate and excluded from every family. A
morning can be blocked by an input that cannot change the call."* Consequence: *"eight of the nine inputs can block
and only six can vote"* (review summary: `votes 8` rows, `distinct_vote_series 6`, `required_not_voting_rows 2`).

**v0.1.1 proposes:** state the requirement as an operand of the spread, not a ninth standalone gate. The nine-input
list is re-declared as **eight inputs plus one named operand**: `spread` is computed from DGS2 and DGS10, so the
presence test for `front` survives **inside** the spread row instead of beside it, and the gate count matches the vote
set.

**Why:** the gate currently blocks on data it never uses, which inflates blockers on a sample of zero and invites the
reading that nine series drive the call. Naming the operand keeps every bit of evidence required and stops the
miscount.

**Verification:** the H1.1 contract check must report `inputs 8`, `operands ["front"]`, and reproduce the same
`spread` value; the same fixture set must produce identical `spread` votes; `contract_check.ok` must still be `true`.
Coverage rung counts drop by one rung (`front` stops being a standalone rung), so the coverage header and the "n of
nine" wording must change in the same commit.

**Default if no reply:** accept the rulebook wording change, defer the count change, because a count change renumbers
the coverage header that the published screen and the review both quote. Marked `deferred` in the JSON mirror.

**Risk:** a thin identity change that looks cosmetic but touches every count the screen publishes; do it once, with the
screen text, not in two passes.

## 3. P-03 · The VIX row: level-only or a change feature (H1R-02, severity *gap*)

**v0.1.0 records:** *"The row names VIX level and change at the cutoff. The executable reads the level only; no VIX
change is among the nine inputs."* Consequence: *"The regime gate cannot react to a VIX move, only to its level."*

**v0.1.1 proposes:** **retitle the row as level-only** (default), and record the change feature as a v0.2 candidate.
Adding a one-day change means a second VIX observation with its own three clocks, a new threshold and a new input
slot: three unmeasured decisions bundled into a version patch.

**Why:** the row claims more than the code does, which is the exact class of defect this review exists to find.
Retitling makes the declaration true today and costs no evidence. The change feature is a real hypothesis, but its
threshold would be a guess presented as a rule.

**Verification:** the rulebook row text changes; the model output, fixture digests and coverage counts must not change
at all (a pure text patch). If the owner instead chooses the change feature, it enters as a tenth input with its own
96-hour cap and rung, the fixture set is regenerated, and the version becomes v0.2, not v0.1.1.

**Default if no reply:** retitle as level-only.


## 4. P-04 · F23 distance: declare it unimplemented (H1R-04, severity *gap*)

**v0.1.0 records:** *"Two calendar rows share one input. The event gate counts releases in [cutoff, endpoint); distance
since or until an event has no executable representation."* Consequence: *"The count is the only calendar feature, so
'shared F23' is a declaration rather than an implemented rule."*

**v0.1.1 proposes:** record F23 as **unimplemented** in the rulebook table, with the reason, rather than inventing a
distance field. The distance feature needs an anchor (last release or next release), a unit (hours or sessions) and a
threshold; none is measured, and the archive's calendar is incomplete anyway (P-06).

**Verification:** the rulebook table gains an `unimplemented` value for F23; the `shared_evidence` table must show one
implemented feature and one declared-only feature; the event-gate count, the vote arithmetic and the coverage counts
must be unchanged.

**Default if no reply:** declare unimplemented.

## 5. P-05 · Trace field name and the counted vote set (H1R-06, severity *presentation*)

**v0.1.0 records:** *"The trace carries votes.front even though frontCounted is false and no family reads it. A reader
adding the trace's votes would double count the US leg."*

**v0.1.1 proposes:** rename the trace field `votes.front` to `frontReportedOnly`, and publish
`counted_votes: ["spread", "real", "broad", "ice", "gold", "qqq"]` beside the family means. `frontCounted: false` stays
for continuity.

**Why:** a trace is read as the explanation of the call; a field named like a vote and ignored by the arithmetic is a
silent mis-explanation. This is the cheapest entry in the set and the one a reader meets first when opening a morning.

**Verification:** the fixture trace changes shape, so `expected.json` is regenerated **inside the v0.1.1 package only**;
a guard test asserts the counted set has six members and that the mean of the family means reproduces `score`; the
screen's factor table reads the new key and shows "reported only" on the front row.

**Default if no reply:** accept.

## 6. P-06 · Calendar admissibility wording (H1R-10, severity *gap*)

**v0.1.0 records:** *"The event gate is only as good as the archived schedule. The staged calendar carries FOMC
statements and none of the four other required release families."* Consequence: *"A zero count cannot be read as 'no
release occurred' until the omitted schedules are archived."*

**v0.1.1 proposes:** add one admissibility sentence to the rulebook: **"a zero event count computed from an incomplete
calendar is not evidence that no release occurred; it blocks the morning."** This is a rulebook statement, not code:
the coverage audit already blocks all 725 mornings because `calendar.schedule_complete` is 0, and the sentence makes
that block intentional and legible instead of an accident of a missing file.

**Verification:** the rulebook row carries the sentence; the coverage audit keeps `calendar` in the blocker list; no
arithmetic change. When the four missing families are archived, `schedule_complete` flips to 1 and the rule is
satisfied without a code edit.

**Default if no reply:** accept.


## 7. Entries deliberately left out

- **H1R-05 (no fallback is a choice)** and **H1R-07 (thresholds unvalidated)** are *assumptions*, accepted as declared
  limits of the design. Adding a fallback or calibrating thresholds would be a new hypothesis, not a correction, and
  both would raise the false confidence the screen exists to prevent.
- **H1R-08 (shared evidence reduces votes)** is an arithmetic property of the book, not a defect: six series, three
  families, equal weights. It is reported in the architecture card and needs no rule change.
- **H1R-09 (trace rows are not measurements)** is a reading instruction. The screen already says that a complete trace
  means every row is present and labelled, not measured; v0.1.1 adds that phrase to the trace legend inside P-05's edit.
