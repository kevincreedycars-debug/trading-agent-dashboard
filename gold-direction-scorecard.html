Read this first. This page scores each factor rule in the Gold logic document against the direction Gold actually moved, on intervals the archive already spent. It is not a signal, a proven edge, an accuracy score for the live agent or a trading result. The number that matters is the drift-adjusted edge, not the hit rate: Gold rose on most anchors whatever the factors said, so a rate above 60% can be nothing more than that drift.

The drift floor comes first

A directional call carries no information if it scores exactly the unconditioned rate of the side it called. Every rule below is therefore read as a hit rate minus that side's own baseline for the same cohort and horizon. Because Gold rose on most spent anchors, the floor is high and moves by year, so the same rule can look good in one year and wrong in the next.

Every scored state, and how it behaved year by year

A hit rate is the called side's share of that state's directional outcomes. The drift edge subtracts the same side's unconditioned rate, and a negative edge means the rule leaned the wrong way relative to its own drift. The year strip shows the drift edge for each year the state was observed, so a state that only looks stable after pooling is visible here.
